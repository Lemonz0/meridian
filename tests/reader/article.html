How Engines Paint Text

By Ada Example

Painting text looks simple from the outside, but a browser engine does a surprising amount of work, step by step, before a single glyph reaches the screen.

First, the engine shapes the text: it turns characters into glyphs, applies kerning, ligatures and, for many scripts, contextual forms that depend on neighbouring letters.

Next comes line breaking, which has to respect the language's rules, hyphenation settings, and the width of the box the text lives in, among other things.

Then the engine rasterizes glyphs, usually caching them in an atlas on the GPU, so that drawing the same letter again is cheap and fast.

Finally, the compositor assembles layers. Read this and the follow-up post for the details, including subpixel positioning.
