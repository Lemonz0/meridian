Notes on Caching

Caching is the art of remembering answers, so that the same question, asked again, costs almost nothing to answer the second time around.

A cache needs a policy for forgetting, because memory is finite, and the cost of a wrong guess grows with the size of the working set.

Least-recently-used eviction is a good default, but scan-heavy workloads can flush the useful entries, which is why many systems add a small probationary segment.

Invalidation is the hard part: knowing when a remembered answer is no longer true, without asking the slow source every single time, requires versioning or leases.

Measure hit rates per key class, not just overall, because a high average can hide a hot path that misses constantly and dominates tail latency.
